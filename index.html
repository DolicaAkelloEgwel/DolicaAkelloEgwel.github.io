About Me

I am part of a team of technicians within London College of Communication's Creative Technology Hub, where we work to support students in developing artworks that incorporate various forms of creative tech. The Creative Technology Hub is a space that is available to students from any degree programme and with any level of technical experience -- and we aim to create an environment that fosters experimentation and curiosity. As the Python specialist of the team, my role is centered around supporting students who wish to use Python in some shape or form to further their artistic endeavors. This typically involves delivering workshops, writing up tutorials, and providing one-to-one consultations. I also sometimes help students do work that involves AI and Machine Learning which, according to some, means I am a tool of the Antichrist or that I'm not hot. I'll let you be the judge of that. I often get asked why arts students would want to learn or might benefit from learning Python. Even after three years in this role, I still struggle to give an answer to this question. Tools like p5.js, Touchdesigner and Unreal Engine (all of which you can also learn about at the CTH!) *exist* to create artistic content. Python does not. Answering this question is also made more difficult by the fact that different students tend to have different ideas about what it is they wish to achieve with Python, making it hard for me to define "Creative Python" or to give a summary of it. But this variety, and the terra incognita-ness of what I do, is exactly what makes my work so fascinating to me. My pivot has also taught me that a job needs a certain level of play in order for me to not lose the will to live remain happy there. It also helps that, with being my employer being an arts college and all, it's not totally out of the ordinary to walk past someone wearing a Pan Sonic or Full of Hell shirt, or to bump into coworkers and students at Cafe OTO. As someone that spent a fair chunk of my teenage years probing RYM charts when I probably should have been having those Skins/Euphoria experiences, this is something I really enjoy about working here. In another life I was a Software Engineer at the Science and Technology Facilities Council. I worked in ISIS (not that one...) where I developed programs to help scientists analyse data from and record information about neutron experiments.
