About Me

I am part of a team of technicians within London College of Communication's Creative Technology Hub, where we work to support students in developing artworks that incorporate various forms of creative tech. The Creative Technology Hub is a space that is available to students from any degree programme and with any level of technical experience -- and we aim to create an environment that fosters experimentation and curiosity. As the Python specialist of the team, my role is centered around supporting students who wish to use Python in some shape or form to further their artistic endeavors. This typically involves delivering workshops, writing up tutorials, and providing one-to-one consultations. I also sometimes help students do work that involves AI and Machine Learning which, according to some, means I am a tool of the Antichrist or that I'm not hot. I'll let you be the judge of that.

Something I get asked fairly often is what arts students even do with Python or how they benefit from learning it. Even after three years in this role, this is still a question I find difficult to answer. Tools such as p5.js, TouchDesigner and Unreal Engine (all of which you can also learn about at the CTH!) exist to create artistic content. Python does not. Attempting to summarise the essence of "Artistic Python" is also made more difficult by the fact that different students tend to have different ideas about what it is they wish to achieve with Python. But this variety in the projects I get involved in, and the terra incognita-ness of what I do, is exactly what makes my job so fascinating to me. My career pivot has also taught me that a job needs to have a certain level of play in order for me to not lose the will to live feel engaged at work. Another perk of being an employee of an arts college is that it's totally not out of the ordinary to see someone wearing a Pan Sonic or Full of Hell shirt, or to bump into coworkers and students at Cafe OTO. As someone that spent a fair chunk of my teenage years probing RYM charts when I probably should have been having those Skins/Euphoria experiences, this is something I really enjoy about working here.

In another life I was a Software Engineer at the Science and Technology Facilities Council. I worked in ISIS (not that one...) where I developed programs to help scientists analyse data from and record information about neutron experiments.
